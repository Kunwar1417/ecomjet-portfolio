What they wanted

The brief.

LTX Studio is Lightricks' professional AI video production platform. It converts a script into a finished video, handling scene breakdown, storyboarding, character casting, shot editing, camera control, and post-production inside one workspace. It is not a one-click generator. It is a directed medium, built for people who want creative authorship, not just automation.

They came to me across multiple campaigns, each one spotlighting a different capability as the platform evolved: AI storyboarding, character creation and consistency, shot editing, video model quality, and collaboration. The ask each time was the same: show what this looks like in practice, for someone who actually makes content or runs a business, without making it feel like a product demo. The target audience was creators, marketers, and filmmakers who were already curious about AI video but hadn't found a tool that gave them real control.

How I cracked it

The approach.

The angle: Each reel picked a format the audience already understands and then built it live using LTX Studio. A BMW commercial. A Lay's chip ad. A Batman superhero short. A fully produced YouTube video, twice over. The hook was never "here's an AI tool." The hook was "here's the finished thing, and here's how long it took." The platform became the explanation for how something that looked expensive got made in minutes.

The framework: Open with the output, something polished and recognizable, then pull back to show the process. Viewers watch because they want to know how the finished piece was made. They stay because LTX Studio makes the answer look achievable. The two "in just 10 mins" reels leaned hardest into this, showing that a complete, story-driven YouTube video is within reach for anyone who has something to say.

What I cut: Feature lists and interface walkthroughs. LTX Studio has a deep toolset, but the audience doesn't need to know every capability before they're interested. Show the result first. The tool sells itself once the viewer wants what the reel just produced.

The honest take

What made it work.

The 3M reel and the 1.8M reel used the same concept, a complete YouTube video built in 10 minutes, and both cleared seven figures. That's not a coincidence. The "finished output in minimal time" format resonates with this audience because time is the actual barrier to video production, not talent or ideas. LTX Studio solved for that and the reels made the solution visible.

The 41K links sent at 45% CTR is the other number worth sitting with. That's a lot of intent from an audience watching short-form content. It happened because the reels didn't ask viewers to imagine using the tool, they watched it produce something real on camera. A BMW commercial. A Lay's chip ad. A Batman short. Those are outputs people recognize as valuable, so clicking to try the platform felt like a logical next step, not an ad response.

The consistent 9% engagement across five very different creative formats tells you the approach held up. Whether the reel was loud and cinematic or quiet and educational, the audience stayed and responded. That consistency is what makes LTX Studio a strong fit for this audience: it works across content styles, and the reels proved that without ever needing to say it directly.
